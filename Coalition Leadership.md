It is important, for the long term success of this coalition, that leadership is selected fairly and in a regular enough cycle to ensure that we are well represented, easily reachable, and well organized. To that end, we will have 5 leadership positions; 1 Coalition President, and 4 Coalition Delegates.

In the interest of making sure that we are represented in this current moment, I (Adrian) will serve as Acting President of the Coalition until the first election concludes. The first election will begin as soon as this amendment is ratified, following the election procedures below.

The Acting President serves to keep the coalition running until the selection of an actual President concludes, acting as the point of contact and organizing discussions. The Acting President may not negotiate, make agreements, or create procedures on behalf of the coalition, and may not appoint the election coordinator or influence how the election is run. Any urgent matters must be brought to the full membership for a decision, and any member may bring a disputed interpretation of procedure to the full membership for a decision. The Acting President may be removed at any time through a motion of coalition collective action. If no delegation is present, the longest-standing member of the coalition who is willing to serve is selected as the Acting President.

Annual elections will be held each year, with voting opening on the first Friday of February. The first annual election will open voting on February 4th, 2028. Nominations for annual elections open 17 days before voting opens.

If a seat on the delegation becomes vacant, the remaining delegation must notify the coalition within 48 hours, and election procedures will begin. If a vacancy occurs within 45 days of an annual election, the seat will instead be filled in that election.

Election procedures are as follows:

- An election coordinator is appointed to oversee the election. The coordinator is ineligible to run for an open vacancy. [^1]
- The coordinator announces a schedule for the upcoming election. They’ll create a nominations channel members can use to nominate themselves or other member(s) for the open seat(s). Any member of the coalition who has not previously been removed from office by coalition action or otherwise barred from running by the coalition can volunteer to run. Nominations for special elections must be open for at least 72 hours, while nominations for general elections open 45 days before the day of the vote.
- The election coordinator should announce the full list of candidates and each candidate may make a public statement in an election forum within 24 hours after the nominee confirmation deadline.
- 1 week after the candidates have been announced, the election will take place by secret ballot. Voting lasts 1 week on [https://vote.heliosvoting.org/](https://vote.heliosvoting.org/)
    - Each member may vote for up to as many candidates as there are open seats. The candidates with the most votes fill the open seats. Ties for the final seat are broken by a runoff between the tied candidates that runs for 1 week. This election must be setup
- After 1 week, the election coordinator announces the winner(s) of the election. Incoming delegates have 72 hours to get together to confirm who the president will be, and announce the result to the coalition. Delegates and the president officially take their seats after these 72 hours, and members can use this time before its official to verify or dispute the results of the election.

The role of the president is as follows:

- Interpret current procedures and ensure they are being followed
- Ensure new procedures are being implemented in a timely manner
- Act as primary point of contact and spokesperson for the coalition

The delegates a whole can override any procedural action by a simple majority vote, should they feel the rules are being interpreted incorrectly.

How the delegation chooses to negotiate with third parties is at their discretion. However, while they have been chosen as the voices of the coalition, they do not have the unilateral power to accept any agreements on behalf of the coalition. They must regularly update the coalition of matters of importance, e.g new information, new offers or deals, etc.

The delegation may change the President at any time by majority vote, choosing from among its members. If the members of the delegation choose to remove the current president from their position, the president gets demoted to a member of the delegation. Removal of 1 or more members from the delegation, including the current president, can happen at any time through a motion of coalition collective action, for any reason.

The President may temporarily delegate their duties to another member of the delegation, who serves as President pro tempore. The President must announce this to the coalition, including how long it will last. A President pro tempore may serve for up to 30 days at a time, and the delegation may end the arrangement at any time by majority vote. The delegation may also designate a delegate to be President pro tempore in the event that the president is unreachable for a period of time exceeding 7 days. 

If the President resigns, is removed, or otherwise leaves office, the delegation will select a new President from among its members by majority vote within 72 hours. Until then, the President pro tempore, if one is serving, becomes Acting President. If none is serving, the longest-serving delegate becomes Acting President. If 2 delegates have been serving as delegates for an equal amount of time, then the longest serving member of the coalition between them is the Acting President. If the departing President also leaves the delegation, their seat is filled by special election. If no delegation remains, the longest-standing member of the coalition who is willing to serve becomes Acting President until an election concludes.

Presidents removed from office by a coalition collective action are not eligible to be president in the future. Delegates removed by coalition collective action are also not eligible to be a delegate in the future.

The delegation must maintain accurate records that remain accessible to future classes of delegations. These records include but are not limited to important third party conversations and negotiated deals.

Members are barred from collecting and spending money on behalf of the organization until such time that an amendment can be ratified with financial governance
